The wind-up car with the big wing — and it actually runs

No batteries, no motor. This little car is fully 3D-printed in white PLA on a Bambu Lab A1, and it runs on a wound spring.

Wind it up, let go, and the gear train does the rest: the wheels drive the car across the floor while the big two-blade wing on top spins like a propeller. The wing is the first thing everyone notices — it makes a palm-sized printed car look faintly ridiculous and completely alive at the same time.

The mechanism was designed with AI help (I build most of my models with Claude): gears, spring housing and frame all come out of code, then get printed, assembled, and tested on the floor. Plenty of my printed mechanisms have refused to move over the years, which is exactly why this one matters to me — a mechanism designed in code, printed in plastic, that simply runs when you let go of it.

I filmed it running across my floor, wobbling slightly and spinning its wing the whole way. That video is the kind of proof I want this site to be built on: not renders, not promises — the thing itself, moving.
